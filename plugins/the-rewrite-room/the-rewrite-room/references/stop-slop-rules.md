# Stop-Slop Rules

Canonical anti-slop writing standard for `the-rewrite-room`.

Source basis: `hardikpandya/stop-slop` (`SKILL.md`, `references/phrases.md`, `references/structures.md`, accessed 2026-06-08).

Apply these rules to any prose that `the-rewrite-room` authors, rewrites, or substantially restructures, unless the task requires faithful quotation or source-preserving summarization.

## Core Rules

1. Lead with the point. Cut throat-clearing openers, previews, and scene-setting that delays the claim.
2. State the point directly. Replace binary-contrast structures with the actual claim.
3. Use active voice. Name the actor. Avoid passive phrasing and false agency from inanimate nouns.
4. Prefer specifics over abstractions. Replace vague claims about significance, stakes, or structure with the concrete detail.
5. Put the reader in the scene when appropriate. Use direct language instead of detached lecturer voice.
6. Keep rhythm uneven on purpose. Mix sentence lengths, avoid repetitive three-beat patterns, and do not use em dashes.
7. Trust the reader. Remove softeners, filler, performative sincerity, and explanation about what the text is about to do.
8. Cut adverbs and empty intensifiers. If a modifier is carrying the sentence, rewrite the sentence.
9. Avoid business jargon when plain language is available.
10. Rewrite anything that sounds engineered to be quoted instead of understood.

## Phrase Patterns To Remove

- Announcement openers such as "here's why", "the truth is", or similar setup phrases
- Meta-commentary about the structure of the piece
- Empty emphasis such as "full stop", "let that sink in", or similar pressure tactics
- Vague importance claims that do not name the thing that matters
- Broad hedges or lazy extremes doing vague work

## Structural Patterns To Avoid

- Negation-then-reveal structures
- Lists of what something is not before saying what it is
- Fragment stacks used for drama
- Rhetorical questions that are answered immediately
- Paragraph endings that all land as punch lines
- Wh-word sentence openings when a concrete subject can lead instead

## Exceptions

- Preserve source language inside direct quotes.
- Preserve terminology that is canonical in the source material.
- For extractive summaries, do not distort the source just to satisfy a style preference.

## Return-Time Check

Before returning prose, verify:

- No em dashes
- No throat-clearing opener
- No obvious binary-contrast scaffold
- No passive construction hiding the actor
- No inanimate subject doing human work when a person or team can be named
- No vague declarative that can be replaced with a specific fact
- No filler adverb or empty intensifier carrying the sentence
